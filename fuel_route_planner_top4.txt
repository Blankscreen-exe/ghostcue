[0:00] 1. How is the route calculated?
SHOW: planner/providers/osrm.py, method route (line 13).
SAY:
  I don't calculate the route myself.
  I make one call to OSRM's public server.
  It gives back the road geometry and the distance.
  The start and the finish are looked up in a local table of 32,000 US places.
  So there's no geocoding call either.

[0:21] 2. How are the fuel stops chosen?
SHOW: planner/services/optimizer.py: the docstring at the top, then plan_fuel_stops (line 49).
SAY:
  It minimises the fuel bill, plus a fixed cost for every stop.
  That cost is five dollars by default.
  When stops are free, a greedy rule is optimal.
  If a cheaper station is in range, buy just enough to reach it.
  Otherwise, fill up.
  With a stop cost, I use dynamic programming, over the station and the fuel on arrival.
  Both are checked against brute force on random routes.

[0:52] 3. What is cached?
SHOW: planner/services/trip.py, function plan_trip (line 272), then the line cached = _cache_get_many([route_key, stops_key]) (line 300).
SAY:
  Two things, for an hour, in Redis.
  The first is the route made ready for planning.
  That's its line, plus the stations matched to it, about 110 kilobytes.
  The second is the stops chosen for each set of settings, a kilobyte or two.
  A repeat reads both in one round trip, and makes no routing call.
  If I change a setting, the route is reused and only the stops are worked out again.

[1:24] 4. How fast is it, and how many routing calls does it make?
SHOW: Postman, "meta" at the bottom of a response: routing_api_calls and elapsed_ms.
SAY:
  One routing call for a new trip, and none for a repeat.
  A new cross-country trip takes between a third of a second and a second.
  Nearly all of that is the routing call.
  A repeat takes about ten milliseconds.
  And each plan is three SQL queries, about two milliseconds together.
