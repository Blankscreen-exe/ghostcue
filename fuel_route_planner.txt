SAY:

  Next, which stations are on the route.
  The price file has about 6,600 US stations, and I use all of them.
  It has no coordinates, so each station is placed at the centre of its town.
  They're kept in memory as numpy arrays.
  One matrix multiplication gives every station's distance from the route.
  That takes about fifteen milliseconds for a cross-country trip, with no database query.

[3:58] The optimizer
SHOW: planner/services/optimizer.py. Start on the docstring at the top (the "fuel bill + stop_cost x number of stops" line), then plan_fuel_stops (line 49), then _cheapest_with_stop_cost (line 130).
SAY:
  And this is the optimizer.
  It minimises the fuel bill, plus the stop cost for every stop.
  When stops are free, it's a simple greedy rule.
  If there's a cheaper station in range, buy just enough to reach it.
  If not, fill up.
  When stops have a cost, I use dynamic programming, over the station you're at and the fuel you arrive with.
  Both are tested against brute force on random routes.
